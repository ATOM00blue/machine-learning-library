## Abstract

Quantizing large language models (LLMs) to 1-bit precision significantly reduces computational costs, but existing quantization techniques suffer from noticeable performance degradation when using weight and activation precisions below 4 bits (W4A4). In this paper, we propose a post-training quantization framework with W(1+1)A(1*4) configuration, where weights are quantized to 1 bit with an additional 1 bit for fine-grain grouping and activations are quantized to 1 bit with a 4-fold increase in the number of channels. For weight quantization, we propose utilizing Hessian-aware fine-grained grouping along with an EM-based quantization scheme. For activation quantization, we decompose INT4-quantized activations into a 4 * INT1 format equivalently and simultaneously smooth the scaling factors based on quantization errors, which further reduces the quantization errors in activations. Our method surpasses state-of-the-art (SOTA) LLM quantization baselines on W2A4 across multiple tasks, pushing the boundaries of existing LLM quantization methods toward fully binarized models. ## Why it matters

- Introduces a post-training quantization framework (W(1+1)A(1*4)) that pushes LLM quantization toward full binarization without requiring retraining, dramatically cutting inference costs.
- Proposes Hessian-aware fine-grained grouping combined with an EM-based scheme for 1-bit weight quantization, mitigating the accuracy loss typical at ultra-low bit widths.
- Decomposes INT4 activations into a 4×INT1 format and smooths scaling factors using quantization errors, achieving effective 1-bit activation quantization with reduced degradation.
- Outperforms state-of-the-art W2A4 baselines across multiple tasks, demonstrating that near-binary quantization of large language models is practically achievable.
