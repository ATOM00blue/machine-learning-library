## Abstract

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) enables efficient fine-tuning of large language models but suffers from catastrophic forgetting when learned updates interfere with the dominant singular directions that encode essential pre-trained knowledge. We propose Orthogonal Projection LoRA (OPLoRA), a theoretically grounded approach that prevents this interference through double-sided orthogonal projections. By decomposing frozen weights via SVD, OPLoRA constrains LoRA updates to lie entirely within the orthogonal complement of the top-k singular subspace using projections P_L = I - U_k U_k^⊤ and P_R = I - V_k V_k^⊤. We prove that this construction exactly preserves the top-k singular triples, providing mathematical guarantees for knowledge retention. To quantify subspace interference, we introduce ρ_k, a metric measuring update alignment with dominant directions. Extensive experiments across commonsense reasoning, mathematics, and code generation demonstrate that OPLoRA significantly reduces forgetting while maintaining competitive task-specific performance on LLaMA-2 7B and Qwen2.5 7B, establishing orthogonal projection as an effective mechanism for knowledge preservation in parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Why it matters

- OPLoRA introduces double-sided orthogonal projections (via SVD decomposition) that mathematically guarantee preservation of the top-k singular subspace of pre-trained weights, directly preventing catastrophic forgetting during LoRA fine-tuning.
- The method provides a formal proof that the top-k singular triples are exactly preserved, offering theoretical grounding absent from many prior PEFT approaches.
- A new metric ρ_k is proposed to measure how much a LoRA update aligns with dominant singular directions, providing a diagnostic tool for subspace interference.
- Experiments on LLaMA-2 7B and Qwen2.5 7B across commonsense reasoning, math, and code generation tasks confirm that OPLoRA reduces forgetting while remaining competitive in task-specific performance.
