## Abstract

Model merging is an efficient empowerment technique in the machine learning community that does not require the collection of raw training data and does not require expensive computation. As model merging becomes increasingly prevalent across various fields, it is crucial to understand the available model merging techniques comprehensively. However, there is a significant gap in the literature regarding a systematic and thorough review of these techniques. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of model merging methods and theories, their applications in various domains and settings, and future research directions. Specifically, we first propose a new taxonomic approach that exhaustively discusses existing model merging methods. Secondly, we discuss the application of model merging techniques in large language models, multimodal large language models, and more than ten machine learning subfields, including continual learning, multi-task learning, few-shot learning, etc. Finally, we highlight the remaining challenges of model merging and discuss future research directions.

## Why it matters

- Introduces a new taxonomy of model merging methods, providing the first comprehensive systematic review of this rapidly growing technique in the ML community.
- Covers applications across large language models (LLMs), multimodal LLMs, and over ten ML subfields including continual learning, multi-task learning, and few-shot learning.
- Model merging is highlighted as a compute-efficient and data-free alternative to traditional fine-tuning or retraining, making it highly relevant for practical deployment.
- Identifies open challenges and future research directions, serving as a roadmap for the field.
