## Abstract

Semi-structured N:M sparsity and low-bit quantization (e.g., 1.58-bit BitNet) are two promising approaches for improving the efficiency of large language models (LLMs), yet they have largely been studied in isolation. In this work, we investigate their interaction and show that 1.58-bit BitNet is naturally more compatible with N:M sparsity than full-precision models. To study this effect, we propose Sparse-BitNet, a unified framework that jointly applies 1.58-bit quantization and dynamic N:M sparsification while ensuring stable training for the first time. Across multiple model scales and training regimes (sparse pretraining and dense-to-sparse schedules), 1.58-bit BitNet consistently exhibits smaller performance degradation than full-precision baselines at the same sparsity levels and can tolerate higher structured sparsity before accuracy collapse. Moreover, using our custom sparse tensor core, Sparse-BitNet achieves substantial speedups in both training and inference, reaching up to 1.30X. These results highlight that combining extremely low-bit quantization with semi-structured N:M sparsity is a promising direction for efficient LLMs.

## Why it matters

- Sparse-BitNet is the first unified framework to jointly apply 1.58-bit quantization and dynamic N:M semi-structured sparsity, enabling stable training with both techniques simultaneously.
- 1.58-bit BitNet models are shown to be inherently more compatible with N:M sparsity than full-precision models, suffering less accuracy degradation at equivalent sparsity levels.
- A custom sparse tensor core delivers up to 1.30x speedups in both training and inference, demonstrating real hardware efficiency gains from the combined approach.
- The work establishes that combining extreme low-bit quantization with structured sparsity is a viable and promising direction for building more efficient large language models.
