## Summary

ESM3 is a frontier multimodal generative language model developed by EvolutionaryScale that reasons jointly over protein sequence, structure, and function. Trained at scale on evolutionary data spanning more than 3 billion years of biology encoded in natural proteins, ESM3 can generate functional proteins that are far removed from any known protein. The model can follow complex prompts that combine its three modalities and is highly responsive to alignment techniques that improve generation fidelity.

To demonstrate ESM3's generative reach, the authors prompted it to design novel fluorescent proteins. Among the synthesized candidates, they discovered a bright fluorescent protein with only 58% sequence identity to the closest known fluorescent protein — a distance the authors estimate is equivalent to simulating 500 million years of natural evolution. This result shows that a language model trained on evolutionary data can navigate protein space well beyond what natural evolution has explored within experimentally tractable timescales.

The work establishes that large-scale language models, when trained on the right evolutionary corpus and equipped with multimodal reasoning over sequence, structure, and function, are capable of true de novo protein generation rather than incremental variation. ESM3 represents a new paradigm for computational protein design with direct implications for drug discovery, synthetic biology, and our understanding of protein fitness landscapes.

Published in *Science*, Vol. 387, Issue 6736, pp. 850–858 (2025). PMID: 39818825.

## Key points

- ESM3 is a multimodal generative language model trained on protein sequence, structure, and function that can generate proteins far from the natural distribution.
- The model generated a novel fluorescent protein at 58% sequence identity from known fluorescent proteins, estimated to represent 500 million years of simulated evolution.
- ESM3 supports complex multi-modal prompting and is responsive to alignment techniques for improved generation fidelity.
- The approach demonstrates that evolutionary-scale language models can serve as generative engines for de novo protein design.
