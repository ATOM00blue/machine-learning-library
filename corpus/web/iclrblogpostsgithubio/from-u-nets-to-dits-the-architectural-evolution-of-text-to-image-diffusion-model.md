## Summary

This comprehensive ICLR 2026 blog post traces the architectural evolution of text-to-image diffusion models over five years (2021–2025). The article examines the transition from U-Net-based architectures — including foundational models like Stable Diffusion — to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). Key developments covered include SDXL's scaling advances, efficiency innovations like Stable Cascade, and transformer-based breakthroughs like PixArt-α and Flux.1-Dev.

The central thesis is that "scaling favors attention," explaining why transformer architectures outperformed convolutional networks as computational demands and model sizes increased. The post provides detailed architectural descriptions, parameter comparisons across models, and experimental evaluations supporting this claim. Future directions discussed emphasize image editing and multimodal applications.

The article concludes with perspectives on competing paradigms such as autoregressive models, acknowledging that hardware evolution played a significant role in enabling architectural transitions. The knowledge cutoff for the survey is November 17, 2025, capturing the full arc from the original latent diffusion model era through the dominance of DiT-based systems like Flux.

## Key points

- Traces the full U-Net → DiT architectural shift in text-to-image generation across 2021–2025
- Argues that attention mechanisms scale more efficiently than convolutions as model size and compute grow
- Covers landmark models: Stable Diffusion, SDXL, Stable Cascade, PixArt-α, and Flux.1-Dev with parameter comparisons
- Discusses future directions including image editing, multimodal integration, and competition from autoregressive models
